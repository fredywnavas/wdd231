About Public Domain Films

What is Public Domain Film?

A public domain film is a movie that is no longer protected by copyright (or never was properly protected), allowing anyone to:

  • Watch it legally for free
  • Download and share copies
  • Restore and preserve it
  • Use clips in projects without obtaining permission
  • Create derivative works (subject to any remaining rights such as trademarks or music copyrights)

A Common Misconception

Just because a movie is old doesn't mean it's public domain.

For example:

  • Gone with the Wind (1939) — still copyrighted.
  • The Wizard of Oz (1939) — still copyrighted.
  • Casablanca (1942) — still copyrighted.

Age alone doesn’t determine public-domain status.

Why Do Films Enter the Public Domain?

Common reasons include:

    1. Copyright expired
      • In the United States, many films released before 1931 are now in the public domain.
      • Additional years enter the public domain every January 1.

    2. Failure to renew copyright
      • Many older films lost protection because studios failed to renew their copyrights under earlier laws.

    3. Improper copyright notice
      • Some films were released without the required notice and immediately entered the public domain.

Interestingly, Night of the Living Dead became public domain because the distributor accidentally removed the copyright notice when changing the title before release.
